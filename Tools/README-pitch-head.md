# The learned pitch head

## What it is

A 940 → 192 → 940 network that reads `PitchTracker.correlationCurve` and answers
which lag belongs to the singer. It does not measure pitch — the autocorrelation
front end does that, to 0.0 cents on a clean sung vowel — it *chooses*, which is
the one thing autocorrelation cannot do and the reason a KTV microphone with the
backing track in it loses every note.

Measured on `PitchAccuracyTests`, the same ruler both are judged by:

| | rule | head alone | combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean sung vowel | 0.0¢ | 13.3¢ | **0.1¢** |
| backing at the voice's level | **5/5 notes lost, 1902¢** | 0/5, 6¢ | **0/5, 3¢** |

Combined means: the head chooses the periodicity, and the rule's own parabolic
interpolation places it exactly. Neither alone is better than both.

## What it is worth end to end

Per-frame numbers are not what a singer sees, so the whole pipeline was measured
as well — a tune sung correctly with an accompaniment underneath it, through
`SingerPitch` and `KaraokeScore` exactly as a performance is, with the head on
and with `YUNAUDIO_NO_LEARNED_PITCH=1`:

| backing level | with head | no head |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0× the voice | 95% | 95% |
| 1.5× | **95%** | 92% |
| 2.0× | **95%** | 89% |
| 3.0× | **70%** | 57% |
| 4.0× | 40% | 43% |

Two things in there are worth saying plainly rather than burying.

**Below the singer's own level it is worth nothing**, and the first two
end-to-end fixtures showed exactly that. One of them was accidentally kind — its
chord was rooted an exact octave under the melody, and `KaraokeScore` forgives
octaves, so following the accompaniment still scored as following the tune. The
per-frame failure was real and its consequence was not. That fixture was replaced
rather than kept.

**And at four times the level both collapse.** No choice among periodicities can
recover a voice that is buried; the honest reading is that the head extends the
range where scoring works, it does not remove the limit.

Where it earns its place is a backing track louder than the singer, which is a
KTV room with the PA up — three, six and thirteen points across that band. The
two unambiguous levels are asserted in `LearnedPitchTests`, so removing the head
or retraining it worse fails a test rather than quietly costing somebody their
score in a loud room.

## Why it is not on the Neural Engine

Because it is too small to pay for the trip, and that is measured rather than
assumed. Per frame on this machine: CPU only 0.051 ms, CPU and ANE 0.049, CPU and
GPU 0.047, all 0.045 — four ways of saying the same number.

The hardware is fine, which was checked with a control rather than argued: eight
3×3 convolutions over 256 channels at 64×64 go from **6.17 ms on the CPU to 2.64
ms on the ANE**. The Neural Engine works here. A 362,000-parameter model is below
the size where dispatching to it wins, and that follows directly from putting a
DSP front end in front of it — which is also what makes the model small enough to
train on synthetic data at all. Reaching the ANE would mean an end-to-end model
over raw audio, which is a different design with a different cost.

## Rebuilding it

Nothing here is needed to build or run the application; the compiled model is
checked in at `Sources/YunAudioEngine/Resources/PitchHead.mlpackage` (720 KB).

    swift build -c release --product yunaudio-pitchdata
    ./.build/release/yunaudio-pitchdata 80000 /tmp/pitchdata
    python3 -m venv /tmp/ane && /tmp/ane/bin/pip install coremltools numpy
    /tmp/ane/bin/python Tools/train-pitch-head.py
    /tmp/ane/bin/python Tools/export-pitch-head.py
    cp -R /tmp/PitchHead.mlpackage Sources/YunAudioEngine/Resources/
    swift test --filter LearnedPitchTests

`coremltools` needs Python 3.12 or older: on 3.14 the conversion fails with
`BlobWriter not loaded`, because the native extension has no wheel for it.

## The data

Synthetic, generated by `Sources/yunaudio-pitchdata`, and no external dataset is
used or needed. Eighty thousand frames of 2048 samples at 48 kHz:

- **the voice** — f0 log-uniform across the whole range the tracker searches,
  four to fourteen partials, up to 0.15% inharmonicity, random phase;
- **the room** — three quarters of frames carry an accompaniment, root 65–330 Hz,
  a chord of 1 / 1.26 / 1.5 / 2.0 with each note present four times in five, at
  **0.2 to 1.6 times the singer's level**, deliberately including louder than the
  singer;
- **noise** — broadband, up to 6%.

Labels are exact by construction: a harmonic stack built at 261.63 Hz is 261.63
Hz. The target is a smoothed distribution over lag bins rather than a one-hot,
because the true period is rarely a whole number of samples, and a regression
head would average two candidates and land between them — the one answer that is
certainly wrong.

**The features come from the Swift front end**, not from a Python reimplementation
of it. Training and inference read the same code, so they cannot drift.

## What it has not seen

A real voice, a real room, a real speaker. It knows the decision problem, not the
world. Breath, plosives, glottal noise and the particular way a PA leaks into a
cardioid are all absent from the training set. That is why the rule's answer is
still taken whenever the two agree, and why the honest next step — if this is
ever not enough — is real recordings rather than a larger synthetic set.

One coverage bug has already been caught this way and is worth remembering: the
first model was trained from 80 Hz upwards, and a test that sings a C2 at 65.41 Hz
found it answering an octave out. A model is only defined where it was shown
something.
